Dump Trailers for Sale

Half-round, frame, and frameless configurations built for construction, aggregate, and scrap operations across Florida and Georgia.

A dump trailer earns its keep by cycling fast and carrying every legal pound it can. Whether you're moving sand and gravel to a job site or running demolition debris to a transfer station, the right unit comes down to payload, body geometry, and how it sheds material. Industries & Applications

Dump trailers move loose material in volume. The body style and capacity you choose should follow the density and abrasiveness of what you haul most.

Construction & Site Development

Hauling fill dirt, sand, base rock, and asphalt millings to grading and paving crews. Frameless and half-round bodies dump fast and stay stable on uneven ground.

Aggregate & Quarry

Moving crushed stone, gravel, and sand from pit to plant. High-density loads reward lighter aluminum bodies that protect legal payload on every trip.

Demolition & Scrap

Concrete, rebar, and mixed C&D debris are hard on steel. Heavier-gauge steel bodies and reinforced floors absorb the abuse that wrecks lighter builds.

Agriculture & Land Clearing

Grain, mulch, citrus, and cleared brush across Central Florida and South Georgia. High-cube bodies make the most of low-density, bulky material.

What to Consider When Buying a Dump Trailer

Spec'ing a dump trailer is a balance between durability and weight. Every pound of trailer is a pound you can't bill, so the goal is matching the build to your load without over-building it.

Capacity & Body Geometry

Capacity is measured in cubic yards, but the body shape matters as much as the number. Half-round (rounded-bottom) bodies clean out completely, resist sticking with wet material like clay or asphalt, and carry a lower center of gravity for safer dumping. Square sheet-and-post bodies hold more cube for light, bulky loads but trap sticky material in the corners. Match the body to whether you're volume-limited or weight-limited.

Axle Configuration

Tandem axles are the standard for most over-the-road dump work and keep tare weight down. Tridem (three-axle) and spread configurations raise your legal gross weight where bridge laws allow it, which pays off on dense aggregate. Confirm how the configuration interacts with Florida and Georgia weight limits before you commit — the right axle count is the one that lets you load to the legal max for your lanes.

Suspension

Air-ride suspension protects both the load and the trailer over rough job-site approaches and is worth it if you run long highway miles. Spring (mechanical) suspension costs less, carries abuse well, and is common on dedicated off-road and short-haul units. If you raise the body to dump, air suspension also gives you a more stable, level platform.

Body Material: Steel vs. Aluminum

Steel bodies take the punishment of concrete, riprap, and scrap without denting, and they're cheaper to repair in the field. Aluminum saves several thousand pounds of tare weight — weight you convert directly into payload on dense loads — and won't rust, which matters in Florida's humidity and coastal salt air. Many fleets run aluminum for aggregate and steel for demolition. A liner or hardened floor is a cheap way to extend a steel body's life.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a half-round and a square dump body?

A half-round (rounded-bottom) body sheds sticky material, cleans out completely, and rides with a lower center of gravity for safer dumping. Square sheet-and-post bodies hold more cube for light, bulky loads but trap material in the corners. Run half-round for wet or sticky aggregate, square for high-volume light material.

Should I run aluminum or steel for aggregate?

Aluminum saves several thousand pounds of tare weight, which converts straight to legal payload on dense aggregate, and it won't rust in Florida humidity. Steel takes the abuse of riprap, concrete, and demolition without denting and is cheaper to repair. Many fleets run aluminum for aggregate and keep steel for scrap.

Do I need a tridem axle or is tandem enough?

Tandem keeps tare weight down and covers most over-the-road dump work. Tridem and spread configurations raise your legal gross weight where bridge laws allow, which pays off on dense loads. Decide based on the heaviest legal load you want to carry in your lanes, then confirm against Florida and Georgia weight limits.

Is air-ride suspension worth it on a dump trailer?

If you run highway miles, yes. Air-ride protects the trailer over rough job-site approaches and gives a more stable, level platform when the body is raised to dump. Spring suspension costs less and absorbs abuse well, which is why it stays common on dedicated off-road and short-haul units.
